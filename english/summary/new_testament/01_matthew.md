# Matthew – Chapter Summaries

**Chapter 1:** The genealogy of Jesus Christ, tracing his line from Abraham through David and the kings of Judah to Joseph. Then the announcement of Jesus' conception by the Holy Spirit, and Joseph's dream in which an angel tells him to take Mary as his wife, citing the prophecy of "Immanuel" (God with us).

**Chapter 2:** The visit of the wise men from the east to Bethlehem, led by the star, bringing gifts of gold, frankincense, and myrrh. Herod's plot to kill the child, the flight of Joseph, Mary, and Jesus to Egypt, and their return after Herod's death to settle in Nazareth.

**Chapter 3:** John the Baptizer's ministry in the wilderness of Judea, calling for repentance. Jesus' baptism in the Jordan, at which the Spirit descends like a dove and the Father's voice confirms Jesus as his beloved Son.

**Chapter 4:** Jesus' temptation in the wilderness over forty days, with the devil offering him bread, a test of God's protection, and all the kingdoms of the world. Jesus rebukes each with Scripture. Then his call of the first disciples (the four fishermen) and his beginning of ministry in Galilee.

**Chapter 5:** The Beatitudes and Jesus' teaching on being salt and light, fulfilling the law, and righteous anger, lust, divorce, oaths, resistance to evil, and loving enemies.

**Chapter 6:** Teaching on giving, praying, and fasting in secret, the Lord's Prayer, treasures in heaven, the single eye, serving God rather than money, and not being anxious but seeking God's kingdom first.

**Chapter 7:** Do not judge, the narrow gate, false prophets known by their fruits, the two houses (rock and sand), and doing the Father's will as the measure of true discipleship.

**Chapter 8:** Healings of a leper and a centurion's servant, calming of a storm, and the driving out of demons into a herd of pigs in the country of the Gergesenes.

**Chapter 9:** Healing of a paralytic, calling of Matthew the tax collector, healing of a woman with a hemorrhage and a ruler's dead daughter, healing of two blind men and a mute, and Jesus' compassion for the crowds.

**Chapter 10:** Jesus sends out the twelve apostles with authority to heal and cast out demons, with instructions on mission, conduct, and what to expect from rejection and persecution.

**Chapter 11:** John's message from prison asking if Jesus is the coming one; Jesus' praise of John, his denunciation of unrepentant cities, his thanksgiving to the Father, and his invitation to the weary to take his easy yoke.

**Chapter 12:** Sabbath controversies (plucking grain, healing a withered hand), the accusation that Jesus casts out demons by Beelzebul, the unforgivable blasphemy against the Spirit, the "sign of Jonah," and Jesus declaring his true family to be those who do his Father's will.

**Chapter 13:** The parable of the sower and its explanation, plus further parables of the darnel weeds, mustard seed, yeast, hidden treasure, pearl of great price, and fishing net. Rejection of Jesus by his own townspeople.

**Chapter 14:** The execution of John the Baptist by Herod. Jesus' feeding of the five thousand, walking on water, and Peter's brief walk on the sea before sinking.

**Chapter 15:** Jesus' critique of the Pharisees' traditions as voiding God's commandment to honor parents. Teaching on what defiles (from the heart, not from the mouth). The Canaanite woman's daughter healed, and the feeding of four thousand.

**Chapter 16:** Peter's confession that Jesus is the Christ, Jesus' promise to build his church on that rock and give Peter the keys. Jesus foretells his passion; Peter rebukes him and is called "Satan." The call to take up one's cross.

**Chapter 17:** The Transfiguration, with Moses and Elijah appearing and the Father's voice commanding to "listen to him." Jesus heals a boy with epilepsy and teaches about prayer and fasting, then pays the temple tax via a fish in Peter's net.

**Chapter 18:** Jesus on humility as children, the seriousness of causing little ones to stumble, the parable of the lost sheep, a procedure for resolving disputes among believers, and the parable of the unforgiving servant teaching the need to forgive.

**Chapter 19:** Teaching on marriage and divorce, allowing children to come, the rich young ruler who went away sad because of his possessions, and Jesus' warning about wealth and salvation.

**Chapter 20:** The parable of the workers in the vineyard illustrating God's generous grace. Jesus again predicts his passion. The Zebedees' request for exalted positions rebuked, teaching servant-leadership.

**Chapter 21:** The triumphal entry into Jerusalem on a donkey, cleansing of the temple, cursing the fig tree, and parables of the two sons and the wicked tenants, pointing to the leaders' rejection of Jesus.

**Chapter 22:** The parable of the wedding feast, Jesus' evasive answer on paying taxes to Caesar, his refutation of the Sadducees on resurrection, the greatest commandment (love God, love neighbor), and the challenge about the Christ being David's Lord.

**Chapter 23:** A strong denunciation of the scribes and Pharisees for hypocrisy, heavy burdens, love of honor, and excluding the kingdom, ending with lament over Jerusalem and the prediction that its house will be left desolate.

**Chapter 24:** Jesus predicts the temple's destruction and the signs of the end: wars, famine, earthquakes, persecution, false prophets, the "abomination of desolation," and his coming in glory. Exhortation to watch, illustrated with parables of the fig tree and the faithful/evil servant.

**Chapter 25:** The parables of the ten virgins, the talents (wise servants rewarded, unprofitable servant punished), and the final judgment in which the nations are separated as sheep and goats based on their treatment of the least.

**Chapter 26:** The plot against Jesus, a woman anointing him in Bethany, Judas' betrayal for thirty pieces of silver, the Last Supper with the institution of bread and wine, Jesus' prediction of the disciples' flight, and his arrest and prayer at Gethsemane.

**Chapter 27:** Judas' remorse and suicide; the trials before the high priest and Pilate; the mocking, scourging, and crucifixion; darkness, Jesus' cry of dereliction, and the cosmic signs of his death. Joseph of Arimathea buries the body, and the tomb is sealed.

**Chapter 28:** The empty tomb, the angel's announcement of the resurrection, Jesus' appearance to the women and to the eleven in Galilee (where some doubted), and the Great Commission: make disciples of all nations, baptizing in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, with Jesus' promise to be with them always.
